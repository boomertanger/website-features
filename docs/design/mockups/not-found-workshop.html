404 page: The workshop, four levels

The approved workshop, then three versions that build on it, each creepier, more realistic and more detailed than the last. The firefly follows your mouse (it wanders on its own when you stop) and its glow is the only light. From level 2 there's an old bubble-screen TV on the bench with a red standby light: click the TV to switch it on and it shows 404, lighting up its corner of the room. What each level adds

  • 1 Workshop (approved). Pegboard with the empty cleaver outline and "gone?", chainsaw, jar of eyeballs, breaker box with the blown fuse, dead bulb, axe, sledgehammer. Roaches, a centipede, a spider.
  • 2 After hours. Adds the CRT TV on the bench (click it), a rusty pipe along the ceiling with a slow drip onto the bench, water stains and a crack down the blocks, a fly-paper strip full of dead flies, cardboard boxes, a sprung mousetrap, grime on the floor, dust drifting in the light and a fine film grain. A rat and more bugs.
  • 3 Someone's been here. Adds a corkboard of Polaroids with the faces scratched out, joined by red string, under "WHO'S NEXT?"; a gas mask where the chain was; a second empty outline ("this one too"); a porcelain doll on the breaker box whose head slowly turns to follow the firefly; a chair facing the wall; "IT'S STILL HERE" and tally marks scratched under the bench; a small swarm of roaches, more eyes, a second spider.
  • 4 Don't stay. Adds hanging plastic sheeting with grimy handprints, and a tall figure behind it that you only glimpse at the edge of the light; shine the light on it and it's gone, then it's somewhere else. Bare footprints lead to the plastic, meat hooks hang from the pipe, "DON'T STAY" is scratched on the wall, and the TV won't stay off: switch it off and it turns itself back on, and once, its 404 becomes "BEHIND YOU".

Still true at every level

  • The text never hides. Title, address, Did you mean and every button sit outside the dark and work without the scene (decorative, aria-hidden).
  • The TV is a toy, not a control. Nothing important is behind it; the page's own 404 message is always in the text. Its light is a second light: crawlers flee it and eyes vanish near it too.
  • No gore. Rust, grime, bugs, tools, shapes in the dark; no blood.
  • Flash safety. The TV warms up as a line that opens out, then static fades to the 404; nothing flashes more than 3 times a second, and the screen is small.
  • Phones: tap the dark to send the firefly, tap the TV to switch it.
  • Reduced motion: the firefly rests in one spot; the TV still switches on, without the warm-up, static or roll; the doll doesn't turn; the figure never appears.
  • Weight: code-drawn SVG and CSS, no images. Level 4 is the largest, around 20 KB gzipped.

Recommendation: 3 Someone's been here

Why 3: it's the most story in the smallest space. The Polaroids, the gas mask, the empty outlines and the doll that turns to watch the light all reward exploring, and the TV gives every visitor a satisfying click that says 404 in the room's own voice. It stays creepy rather than frightening, which suits a page people land on by mistake, including younger viewers.

Why not 4 as the default: the figure and "BEHIND YOU" are the best scare here, but a 404 page is often someone's first visit. My suggestion is to keep 4 as a rare variant (say 1 visit in 10) or for October, so regulars get a surprise without every lost visitor getting jumped.

Level 2 is the safe choice if you want the TV and the realism with no unsettling story at all.
