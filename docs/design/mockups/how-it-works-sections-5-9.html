How it works: sections 5 to 9

Two ideas for each section, in the same language as the new sections 1 and 4: big glowing icon tiles, the cursor spotlight, and things that respond when you point at them. Sections deliberately vary their shape (a diagram, collectibles, a placard, an index, a conversation) so the page has rhythm instead of five more card grids. Hover and click everything; switch Desktop and Phone in the bar.

5Who does what

Today: five rows in a list. The roles relate to each other (ideas flow from players to the Keeper to Boomertanger), and a list can't show that.

Chosen: A, Flow. It shows how the Arcade actually works: players' ideas go to the Keeper, who takes a plan to Boomertanger; pitchers go straight to Boomertanger with new games; and the mods watch over all of it. Hover a role and its arrows light up with a little signal running along them, the same "point at it and it explains itself" idea as the linked timeline in section 4. On phones it becomes a top-to-bottom flow.

6Badges

Today: four small cards. Badges are collectibles, so they should feel like objects you want.

Chosen: A, Flip medals. Each badge is a glossy medal with a shine; hover (or tap on a phone) and it flips to show how you earn it and what it unlocks. The flip makes them feel collectible and keeps the front clean. B's trophy shelf is lovely too, but its details only live in hover tooltips, which phones don't have.

7Fair play

Today: six small icon rows. These are the rules of the house, so they can look like it.

Chosen: A, House rules. The rules as the instruction placard on an arcade cabinet: a lit "House rules" header with blinking bulbs and six numbered rules, their outlined gold numbers lighting up as you point at them. It's a change of shape after the medals, and the outlined numbers echo the chapter numerals. B keeps the card grid from section 1 if you'd rather everything match.

8Glossary

Today: twelve terms in two columns. A glossary gets used to look one word up.

Chosen: B, Term chips. (A for reference:) Type and the list narrows as you go, with the match highlighted; the letter buttons jump to a letter. It's alphabetical, grouped by first letter like a real index, and it keeps working as the Arcade adds words. Everything stays visible when you're not searching.

9FAQ

Today: a plain accordion.

Chosen: A, Ask BOOMBOT. The questions are yours, as chat bubbles; tap one and BOOMBOT, a yellow robot with glowing purple eyes and a red antenna knob (the same red as the joystick), "thinks" for a moment, then answers. It's the friendliest way to end the page and it sounds like the brand, not a help centre. It's still an accordion underneath (buttons, one open at a time, readable by screen readers). B is the polished classic if you'd rather keep it quiet.
