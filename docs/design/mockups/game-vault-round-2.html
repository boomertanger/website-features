Game Vault: round 2

Your round 1 picks (A2 C2 B1 D1 E2 S2 F1 G2), each pushed further in the How it works style: things you point at come alive, every section has one memorable moment, and the motion always explains something. Built on the current kit from dev (door cards, the power-on wordmark, seg nav, chapter numerals, rank colours), with sample data and stand-in covers.

Hover everything, since most of the design lives in the motion. Each section has chips to switch options; the one marked Recommended is my pick. Try hovering the GAMEVAULT wordmark too: its dial spins the combination and the lamp lights.

1The Vault page

Round 1's A2 put Now playing first. These make arriving at the Vault an event.

A4 Spotlight stage. What's playing now gets a cinema banner: its cover blurred into the background with film grain, the cover itself tilting toward your pointer, the other game he's playing tucked behind it, and a bar for every stream so far. The Vault's counts tick up underneath.

A5 Shelves. Instead of one grid: swipeable shelves for Now playing, Most wanted (huge outlined rank numbers in the leaderboard's gold, silver and orange, with I want this too right there), Boomer's best and The ones that got away. Searching or filtering switches to the grid.

A6 Vault door. A real vault door. When it scrolls into view the wheel spins, the bolts pull back, the lamp lights and the door swings open on what Boomer's playing now. Once per visit; after that it just rests open. The Vault's counts sit beside it.

A6 + A5. The door opens on Now playing, and the shelves below turn browsing into something to scroll through. Scroll down past the door to see them.

Recommended: A6 + A5. The door is the Vault's one big moment: it's the name of the feature, acted out, and it answers “what's he playing?” in the reveal. Shelves then give members a reason to scroll, and Most wanted puts the +1 where people are already looking. Plays once per visit and never under reduced motion, so it can't get in anyone's way.

2Cover cards

Round 1's C2 (art and details), now with something to discover when you point at it. Hover the covers: they tilt and catch the light. The choice here also changes the cards in every other section.

C4 Game case. Each cover is a physical case: a lit spine down the left edge, a gloss streak across the plastic, and the status as a sticker slapped on the corner. Like a shelf of real boxes.

C5 Glass reveal. Point at a cover and a frosted panel slides up with Boomer's one-line verdict. On phones it never hides anything: the details stay under the cover as before.

C6 Evidence marks. The cover tells the story at a glance: caution tape across the ones he abandoned, a slow green pulse round what he's playing, a stamped want count on the wishlist, a gold ribbon on a perfect 10.

C5 + C6. The evidence marks at rest, the verdict on hover.

Recommended: C5 + C6. The marks make the grid scannable without reading (the tape is very this channel), and the verdict on hover rewards curiosity with Boomer's voice. The status colours stay on badges, so the meanings don't change.

3Search and filters

Live: type in the frame. Arrow keys move through the panel, Enter picks, Esc closes, and / jumps here from anywhere. Try:

B3 Command search. Typing opens a panel under the box: matching games with the matched letters lit, filters it thinks you mean (“co” offers Only Co-op games, “fini” offers Only Finished), and Add as the last line, all keyboard-driven.

B4 Gliding filters. Status becomes the kit's seg nav (the lit pill glides between tabs, as on the Arcade bar), active filters become removable tokens, the count ticks, and the cards glide to their new places instead of jumping.

B3 + B4. Both together: the panel for finding one game fast, the gliding grid for browsing.

Recommended: B3 + B4. They solve different jobs. The panel gets you to one game in two keystrokes and turns vague typing into filters; the gliding grid makes filtering feel physical, so you can see what dropped out. Both reuse kit pieces (seg nav, chips, popover).

4Adding a game

Round 1's D1 dialog. Here the checks the server already does become something you watch, and adding a game ends with it locked in the Vault. It's interactive.

D3 Checks you can watch. A three-step rail (Find it, Checks, In the Vault) fills gold like the Arcade's journey. While the server checks, each check ticks off one by one. Then the cover drops in, the dial ring spins shut and the lamp lights.

D4 Live preview. A wider dialog with “How it'll look” on the right: the card exactly as it'll sit in the Vault, with your name on it, updating as you pick.

D3 + D4. The preview while you pick, the checks while it adds, the lock at the end. Phones keep the preview as a small row.

Recommended: D3 + D4. The checks are real work the server does anyway (round 1 spec §3), so showing them costs nothing and explains why some adds go to a mod. Seeing your name on the card before you add it is the reason members will want to.

5The game page

Round 1's E2 banner with the score as a vault dial: its ten segments light one by one when it scrolls into view, in Boomer's gold (the colour the site already uses for his own words). Hover the dots on the stream timeline. Switch to Admin for Edit.

E4 Ambient banner. The blurred cover drifts slowly behind film grain, the light flickers once as the page opens, the cover tilts to your pointer, and the numbers count up. Below: the review, then Every stream, a timeline with a dot per stream sized by its length and a flag where he finished it.

E5 Verdict first. Boomer's verdict is the headline, huge, behind a giant outlined quote mark, signed by the mascot, with the dial beside it. The game's name and cover sit underneath. Reads like a review site.

Recommended: E4. The game stays the star (people arrive looking for the game), the mood comes from its own cover, and the timeline is the piece the Stream Library will fill in later: each dot becomes a link to the VOD. E5 is a great look for a “review of the month” moment on the home page.

6Mod queue

Round 1's F1, staff green. Shown as a mod. The triage deck is interactive: click, or focus the card and press A, R or S.

F3 Triage deck. One add at a time on a big card, with exactly what the server checked (ticks, and the one teal flag that sent it here). Approve flies it off to the right with a green glow, Reject to the left, and the next card rises. Keyboard: A, R, S.

F4 Inbox with checks. The round 1 list, with each row opening to show the checks, and checkboxes for approving several at once.

Recommended: F3. Mods check one game at a time anyway, and the flag tells them the only thing they need to decide. It's quick with a keyboard and satisfying to clear. Bulk approve can come later if the queue ever gets long.

7Latest updates

Round 1's G2 covers, on the home page tile.

G3 Live digest. When another game joins within the hour, its cover slides onto the stack, the count ticks and the row glows gold for a moment.

G4 Fan out. Point at the digest and its covers fan out like a hand of cards.

G3 + G4. Both: it moves when there's news, and opens up when you point at it.

Recommended: G3 + G4. Small, but it's on the home page every member sees, and it makes the Vault feel alive between streams.
