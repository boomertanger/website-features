Fact check

What I changed from your notes

Newfoundlanders know this history cold, so I corrected a few things that would get called out. Worth a second look from you before it goes live.

1939 status. Newfoundland was a dominion, but its self-government was suspended in 1934 and it was run by a Commission of Government appointed from London. The page says "a British dominion of its own, run at the time by a commission appointed from London" instead of "an independent country with its own government".
Who served. The Royal Newfoundland Regiment is the First World War unit (Beaumont-Hamel). In the Second World War Newfoundlanders served in the Newfoundland Overseas Forestry Unit, the 166th (Newfoundland) Field Regiment and 59th Heavy Regiment of the Royal Artillery, 125 (Newfoundland) Squadron RAF, the Royal Navy and the merchant navy. The medals use those.
Bell Island. U-boats attacked many places in North America (the Gulf of St. Lawrence, the U.S. coast). What's usually claimed for Bell Island is narrower: the torpedo that hit the loading pier is often called the only direct German attack on land in North America. The page says that, hedged.
Atlantic Charter. It was agreed aboard warships in Placentia Bay, not signed on land, and it's a joint statement rather than a signed treaty.
Acadians. Removed. The Acadians came from Acadia (today's Nova Scotia and New Brunswick), not from French settlers pushed out of Newfoundland, so the Cajun link doesn't hold here.
Titanic distance. Usually given as about 370 miles south of Newfoundland. I added that the Marconi station at Cape Race picked up its distress calls.
Map. Round 2 traces the coast with about 140 points from real coordinates (the Northern Peninsula, White Bay, Notre Dame Bay, Bonavista, Trinity, Conception, St. Mary's, Placentia and Fortune bays, the Burin Peninsula, Bay d'Espoir, Port au Port and the Bay of Islands), adds Fogo Island, New World Island, Bell Island, St. Pierre and Miquelon, and the Labrador shore across the Strait of Belle Isle. Still simplified, still labelled "not to scale".
Newfoundland dog. The Gander story (Dickin Medal, 2000), Seaman with Lewis and Clark, Byron's Boatswain and Nana in Barrie's Peter Pan are all well documented. I left out the popular legends (the dog that saved Napoleon, Tang and the SS Ethie) because they're hard to verify.
